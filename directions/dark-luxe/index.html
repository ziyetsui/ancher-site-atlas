IVHow I Work
01

Name it in the room.

The conversation a board avoids compounds quietly until it is the only conversation. My job is to say the thing first, on the record, while it is still cheap.

02

Govern for decisions, not minutes.

A board exists to sharpen the few calls that matter, not to ratify what already happened. If the meeting could have been a memo, the governance has already failed.

03

Plan the exit before the crisis.

CEO succession is a process, not an obituary. The healthiest companies rehearse it years early, calmly, while everyone still has the luxury of disagreeing.

04

Make the hard call survivable.

I am not there to soften the decision. I am there so the people who must live with it walk out of the room stronger than they walked in.

IXBefore You Write

Q.Do you take operating or interim roles?

No. I left the operating room on purpose. I am most useful on the other side of the table, where the job is to govern and to counsel, not to run.

Q.What stage of company fits best?

Series B through public — the years when ambition outpaces structure and the board stops being a formality. Earlier than that, I am usually the wrong tool.

Q.How do engagements usually begin?

By introduction, with a single unhurried conversation and no deck. If we are a fit, the shape of the work becomes obvious within the hour.

Q.Will you sit on competing boards?

Never knowingly. I hold a hard line on conflicts and will tell you the moment one appears — including when it costs me the engagement.
