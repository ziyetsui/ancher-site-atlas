How I Work

I'd rather be right and late than fast and loud.

For eight years I've covered technology and culture — the way a new tool quietly rewrites a habit, and the habit rewrites a person. I report slowly because the interesting part is never the launch; it's what's left six months later, after the novelty wears off and the behavior stays.

My work has run in The Atlantic, Wired, and The Guardian, and in 2024 it was a finalist for the National Magazine Award in Essays. But the byline I care about most is the one in a reader's inbox at seven in the morning, read all the way to the end.

A Writing Creed

  1. The story is in the aftermath, not the announcement.

    A launch is a press release. The piece worth writing is what's left six months later — the habit that stayed after the novelty wore off.

  2. No metaphor I can't defend to the person it describes.

    If a sentence wouldn't survive being read aloud to its subject, it's decoration, not reporting. I cut it.

  3. Slow is a method, not an apology.

    I'd rather be right and late than fast and loud. The deadline I keep is the one the truth sets.

  4. The reader at seven in the morning is the only editor that matters.

    Every paragraph earns the next one, or it gets deleted. Attention is the thing I write about; I refuse to waste it.
