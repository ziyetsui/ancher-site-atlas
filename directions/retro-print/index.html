From the Studio

"I want a picture to feel like it was made by a hand, not a feed."

Ten years in, I still start every job the same way: paper on the board, gouache mixed by eye, a pencil before anything goes digital. Most of the work that reaches me — book covers for Penguin, editorial commissions, the odd record sleeve — arrives because an art director wants something that looks touched, not generated.

So I lean into the marks. Ink that pools. Halftone that's slightly off-register. Colour pulled from a 1970s paperback rather than a screen's idea of "vibrant." It reads warm because it is warm — printed editions, sold in small runs, several of them now sold out.

If you're commissioning, I work best with a brief and a deadline and the freedom to draw badly first. Prints are released a few times a year through the shop; covers and editorial I take on by the project. Either way, it ends up on paper.

How I Work

1

Paper before pixels

Every job starts on the board. Digital is for assembly, never for the first idea.

2

Draw badly first

The freedom to make ugly sketches is where the good ones come from.

3

Keep the marks

Pooled ink, off-register halftone — the hand should stay visible in the print.

4

Small runs, made well

I’d rather pull fifty good prints than a thousand careless ones.
