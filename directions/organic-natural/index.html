The work

I make a case for working slowly — on purpose, and out loud.

The things I most admire weren't rushed into being. They were tended, season after season, until they were deep enough to hold weight.

Most of what I write returns to one stubborn idea: that the work worth doing rarely rewards speed. I spent years moving fast — shipping, optimizing, answering the next thing — and what I built was wide but shallow. It didn't hold. So I started over with a slower premise.

My book, The Long Season, is about sustainable craft: how to build a practice that survives the years it takes to be any good. The newsletter, Rooted, is where I think out loud each week with nine thousand readers who are tired of hustle and want something that lasts. And the retreats are where it becomes real — small cohorts, rural quiet, and the kind of unhurried attention you can't fake over a screen.

None of it is about doing less. It's about doing the right things deeply enough that they take root — in your work, and in you.

What I hold

A few things I keep returning to.

Depth over speed

The work worth doing rarely rewards rushing. I'd build one thing deeply before ten things widely.

Seasons, not sprints

A practice has lean years and fruitful ones. The point is to stay rooted long enough to see both.

Quiet is a resource

The best thinking I've done was unhurried and offline. I protect attention the way I'd protect soil.

Write it in the open

Thinking out loud, in public, keeps me honest. The newsletter is a decade of working in daylight.
