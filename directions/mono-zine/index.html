[ how i work ]

Four rules I keep coming back to.

  1. 01 Ship the smallest thing that's real. A v0.1 that one person uses beats a v1.0 nobody sees. Scope down until it's embarrassing, then ship it.
  2. 02 Write the post-mortem before the launch. If I can't say plainly what could break, I don't understand it yet. The breakage notes are the most-read thing I make.
  3. 03 Own the database. Self-host-first, one binary where I can. I'd rather carry a little ops weight than rent my own data back from someone.
  4. 04 No funnel, no course. The writing isn't a marketing layer on top of the work — it is the work. Sell the product, not the dream of the product.

[ about ]

Why I write it all down

Most "build in public" is a highlight reel. I'd rather log the breakage.

I've shipped six small products over eight years. Two of them pay rent. Two of them never found a second user. I write the newsletter the way I keep a build log — plain, dated, specific about what went wrong before I say what worked.

It turns out the post-mortems travel further than the launches. The Readwise-for-RSS write-up got more readers than the thing ever had users. So now the writing isn't a marketing layer on top of the work — it is the work, and the products are just where the notes come from.

If you make things and you're tired of the polished version, the newsletter is for you. No funnel, no course. Just what shipped this week and what broke.
