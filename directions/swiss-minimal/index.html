How I think about scope

Four rules I bring to every engagement.
01

Cutting is a decision, not a failure

The "leave it out" list is the deliverable. Anything you keep should survive the question: what breaks if we don't build it this quarter?

02

One metric, written down

A roadmap that serves three metrics serves none. We name the one number that decides whether the bet worked, before we sequence anything.

03

Sequence beats scope

What you build second is usually worth more than what you build first. Order is strategy. Most teams treat it as logistics.

04

No deck survives contact with the team

I leave a one-page memo, not slideware. If a decision can't be stated in a sentence, it isn't a decision yet.

What does an engagement cost?

The flagship six-week sprint is a fixed fee, scoped on the intro call. Teardowns and advisory are smaller. No hourly billing — you pay for the decision, not the hours.

Do you work async or on-site?

Remote-first, EU hours, with a handful of live working sessions. I can travel for a kickoff if it matters, but most of the work happens in shared docs.

What stage suits you best?

Seed through Series C, when there's a real product and too many directions to chase. If you're pre-product, you don't need me yet — keep talking to users.

What do I get at the end?

A ranked build order, a defended "leave it out" list, and one metric written down. A one-page memo, not a slide deck. Decisions your team can act on Monday.
