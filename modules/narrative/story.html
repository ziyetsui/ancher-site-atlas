Profile

Designing for the messy middle

How Maya Chen went from sketching transit maps on napkins to leading design systems used by eleven million daily riders.

By Maya Chen Product Designer 8 min read

I never set out to design software. My first real project was a paper schedule for a bus route my grandmother rode every Tuesday to the fabric market in Guangzhou. The printed timetable was wrong, so I rebuilt it by hand — and that small act of making something legible has shaped every job since.

Good design rarely arrives as a flash of genius. It accumulates, quietly, from a hundred decisions nobody will ever notice.

At a fifty-person logistics startup, I learned that the interesting problems live in the gaps: the handoff between the warehouse scanner and the dispatch screen, the error state nobody tested. I spent two years there obsessing over those seams, and it taught me to treat constraints as a brief rather than an obstacle.

Today I lead a small team building the component library behind a regional transit app. The work is less glamorous than a redesign reveal and far more durable — a button that behaves correctly outlives any homepage.

About

I make complex systems feel inevitable

The best interface is one a tired commuter can use at 6am without thinking. That bar guides everything I ship.

I work in the unglamorous layer — states, edge cases, and the documentation that keeps a team aligned long after the launch buzz fades. My background spans print, research, and a decade of saying "let's test it with one real user before we argue."

— Maya Chen, Hangzhou
